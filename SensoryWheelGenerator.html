Sensory wheel

The lack of standardisation in sensory characterisation across beverage types makes this project a bit of a conceptual nightmare.

  • The levels of each sensory wheel can differ. Some have two levels and some three.
  • The conceptual categorisation of the levels can be multi-sensory (organolyptic, aroma, mouthfeel, etc.) or even degree of attribution (eg., primary, secondary, fermentation-derived)
  • The part-of-speech can differ. Some use adjectives for higher levels while attribute descriptor level is a noun (eg., level1=spicy, level3=spice). This is even swapped in some cases, so not much consistency within a wheel and between wheels.
    • POV is from assessor or from the product. Perception vs Conception

    This then has implications for language processing. If the part of speech is inconsistent then it makes it difficult to code for, let alone standardise. The most resolved concepts are in food ontology and food chemistry. Matrix effect confounds this. Description of complexity, lack of clarity leading to "complexity". Consistency usually comes from expert panels or strict sensory analysis methods.
